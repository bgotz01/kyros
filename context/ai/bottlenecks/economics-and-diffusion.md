# Economics and Diffusion

**Status: binding, and the constraint most often omitted from technical analysis.**

## The constraint

Capability has run far ahead of realised productivity. The gap between what
deployed models can do and what organisations have restructured to use them for
is large, and it is not primarily a technology gap.

Components:

1. **Absorption lag.** Enterprise process change runs on a multi-year clock.
   Electrification took decades to show up in productivity statistics, because the
   gain required reorganising the factory, not installing the motor. The same
   structure applies.
2. **Verification labour.** See `Bottlenecks · Reliability and Verification`. If
   the human must check the work, the saving is a fraction of the task.
3. **Unit economics of inference.** `Canon · Test-Time Compute` made quality a
   purchasable dial, which means margin now depends on how much thinking each
   query is granted — a genuinely new operating problem.
4. **Integration surface.** Value concentrates where a model touches proprietary
   systems and data. That work is bespoke, slow, and not amenable to the scaling
   that produced the models.
5. **Skill and trust distribution.** Adoption within organisations is wildly
   uneven, and the binding constraint is frequently a manager's willingness to
   accept a machine-produced artefact, not the artefact's quality.

## Why Kyros must weight this

The corpus makes the case repeatedly that the consequence inflection is separate
from and later than the capability inflection —
`Canon · The Release of ChatGPT` most sharply. Diffusion is where paradigm shifts
either convert into economic reality or stall for a decade.

It is also where the largest analytical errors sit in both directions:
overestimating near-term impact (`False Positives · Autonomous Driving Timelines`)
and underestimating cumulative impact by extrapolating from a stalled adoption
curve.

## What would relieve it

- Deployment patterns where the model's output is machine-verified, removing the
  human check from the loop.
- Native rebuilds rather than retrofits — organisations designed around the
  capability instead of bolted onto it. This is the electrification analogy's real
  content, and it implies new entrants capture more value than incumbents.
- Liability and audit infrastructure — see `Bottlenecks · Agency, Tool Use and the
  Action Surface`.

## Screening question

For any candidate: **who has to change their behaviour for this to matter, and
what is that actor's switching cost?** A technically decisive result whose
adoption requires reorganising a regulated institution is a ten-year event, not a
one-year event, and should be dated accordingly.
