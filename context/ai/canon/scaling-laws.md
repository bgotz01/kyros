# Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models

**Kaplan, McCandlish et al. (OpenAI) — arXiv, January 2020.**

Showed that language model loss follows smooth power laws in model size, dataset
size and compute, across many orders of magnitude.

## Inversion

Machine learning progress was assumed to come from architectural insight —
better ideas, published, incrementally. This showed that within a fixed
architecture, performance was *predictable from spend*.

That converts research from a discovery process into an engineering and capital
allocation process. You can forecast the capability of a model you have not
trained. You can therefore justify the expenditure to a board before the result
exists.

## Incentives

Decisive, and almost entirely on the side of concentration. If capability is
purchasable and predictable, the actor with the most capital wins, and the
optimal strategy is to spend early and continuously. This is the paper that
underwrites multi-billion-dollar training runs, the Microsoft–OpenAI structure,
and the hyperscaler capex cycle.

It also inverted the incentive to publish. Once capability is a function of
spend rather than insight, the marginal value of open publication to the leader
falls sharply. The progressive closing of frontier labs after 2020 is downstream
of this paper.

## Inflection

Real, and under-called at the time outside a small circle. Its consequences are
industrial rather than technical: capex, energy demand, chip supply, national
policy, and the shape of the competitive field.

Note the correction: Kaplan's compute-optimal allocation was superseded by
Chinchilla (2022), which found models were substantially under-trained on data
relative to parameters. The *existence* of predictable scaling survived; the
coefficients did not.

## Lesson for Kyros

An inflection can be a **claim about the shape of a curve**, with no new
capability demonstrated at all. These are the most under-weighted events in the
field because they produce no demo.

Also: being right about the phenomenon and wrong about the parameters is normal
and does not invalidate the call. Distinguish the structural claim from its
current calibration.
