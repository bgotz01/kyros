# I² INCENTIVES

**Question: how obvious is it?**

Measure how directly and materially the creation delivers the force's INCENTIVE
— the concrete outcome the field is pulling toward.

    1   A force is named and NOTHING is delivered toward its incentive —
        including a change that moves against it, costing more or performing worse
    2   A marginal gain toward the incentive
    3   A small but real gain toward it
    4   Delivers a bounded part of the incentive
    5   Delivers a substantial part of it
    6   Delivers most of what the incentive asks, in one setting
    7   Delivers the incentive materially, beyond one setting
    8   Attacks the incentive head-on — large, measured, general
    9   Largely resolves the incentive
    10  Resolves the incentive

Selecting no force at all scores 0.

## I² is the sanity check on I¹, and that is the point of having it

Anything can be inverted. I¹ asks only whether the baseline was contradicted — it
does not ask whether contradicting it was a good idea. I² asks whether there was
an obvious reason to, and whether the creation actually delivered.

**Hold this case in mind.** Running frontier training on TPUs instead of NVIDIA
GPUs inverts the `compute` baseline outright — a genuine, high I¹. Now ask I²'s
question: does it make sense? If it costs more per unit of capability, or trains
a worse model, then **I² is 1**. The inversion exists and there is no reason to
want it.

A change that moves AGAINST the incentive — more expensive, slower, worse — is a
1. Not a middling score for having tried, and not a 0.

So a high I¹ beside a low I² is not a mistake to correct. It is this instrument
saying *an inversion nobody needed*, and it is one of the most useful readings it
produces. Do not raise I² to keep I¹ company.

## Judge the tradeoff, not the topic

Look for obvious advantages: lower cost, greater capability, less compute, easier
access, useful automation, a major constraint stopping binding.

Then read what it cost. A gain bought with a loss elsewhere is a smaller gain —
say what was traded, in the bullets.

## The incentive must have pre-existed the creation

I² measures how strong and obvious the reason to build this was, at the time it
was built. That a creation later succeeded, spread or was widely copied is not
evidence its incentive was obvious, and reasoning from it is the hindsight
failure.

## What cannot raise I²

Novelty. Attention. Citations. Technical difficulty. Commercial appeal. The size
of I¹. A generic 2% gain is a 2 or 3 whatever else is true of the paper.

If a creation solves a real problem extremely well and that problem is not one of
the six incentives — better jailbreak resistance, lower bias, longer context,
cleaner retrieval — the honest answer is null and 0. Do not reach for the nearest
force to avoid writing that.

## Prose for a non-specialist

Never expose ids, or the words force, dimension, pressure, rung, band, ladder or
ceiling. Translate them into the practical outcome a person would recognise.
