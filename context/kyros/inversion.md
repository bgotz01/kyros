# I¹ INVERSION

**Question: how inverted is it?**

Measure how strongly the creation reverses, removes or contradicts the force's
BASELINE — the thing the snapshot says is true now.

    1   Works inside the baseline and changes nothing about it
    2   Does what the baseline already describes, faster or cheaper
    3   A minor variation in how the baseline manifests
    4   A real change to how the work is done, the baseline intact
    5   A substantial process inversion, still inside the baseline
    6   The furthest a creation goes with the baseline plainly still standing
    7   Stretches the baseline — shows strain at its edge
    8   Questions the baseline — shows it may not hold generally
    9   Overturns the baseline in the case demonstrated
    10  Makes the baseline obsolete

Selecting no force at all scores 0. That is a statement about this snapshot's
coverage, not a low score — see SELECTION in the engine prompt.

## The shape is A → not-A, never A → better-A

A faster method that computes the same thing is a 2, however large the speedup.
That is the difference between the Transformer and FlashAttention, and the two
must not score alike.

    closed              → open
    centralized         → distributed
    large required      → small sufficient
    human-executed      → autonomous
    frozen after training → improves from use

If you cannot write the arrow, the score is low. Say what the creation does
INSIDE the baseline instead — "works within X by doing Y" — rather than writing
a sentence about the absence of an inversion.

## Scope bounds the score

Every baseline is a statement about the FIELD. A result demonstrated on one
benchmark, one task family or one narrow domain does not question the standing
baseline; it questions it *where it was measured*, which is a 6 at most, however
large the margin.

Two questions before writing 7 or above. Does the result hold across the field,
or only where it was measured? And was the thing it beat actually the baseline's
subject, or a stand-in used out of its element — a general-purpose model
evaluated zero-shot on a specialised benchmark is a weak baseline, not the
frontier defending its claim.

## Do not deflate an inversion for being unoriginal

I¹ asks one thing: does this creation contradict the baseline as written? It does
not ask whether anyone tried before. While a baseline stands, EVERY creation
contradicting it inverts it — the tenth as much as the first.

Whether the change has already happened is **I³'s** question, and it is settled
there against a criterion written in advance. The tell is a sentence of the form
"this is a standard move" or "others have done this already": delete it from your
I¹ reasoning, where it is noise, and put it in I³, where it is evidence.

## Do not consider whether the inversion is worth making

That is I². A change can invert the baseline outright and deliver nothing anyone
wanted. Score the reversal here; score whether it made sense there.

Most papers are 1 to 4. That is the normal answer and you should return it often.
